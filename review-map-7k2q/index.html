Part 2 · The Year Plan

More sports. Less burnout.

Kids who play more than one sport do better over time than kids who play tournaments all year.

01

Fall · Sep to Nov

Build skills before the season

Prep and skills
  • Skill work 3 times a week: footwork and weak hand.
  • Side to side movement and speed training 2 times a week.
  • Zero 5 game showcase tournaments.
02

Winter · Dec to Feb

Focus on the school team

Team season
  • Full focus on team role, defense and earning the coach's trust.
  • Keep the car ride home calm. No sideline critiques.
  • Bring energy and good body language on the bench.
03

Spring · Mar to May

Pick your tournaments carefully

Careful AAU
  • No more than 2 weekend tournaments a month.
  • At least 1 practice for every game. 2 is better.
  • Walk away from programs that play 6 games in 48 hours.
04

Summer · Jun to Aug

4 weeks off and other sports

Required rest
  • 4 weeks in a row: put the basketball down.
  • Play another sport: track, swimming, soccer or football.
  • Let the body heal and the hunger for the game come back.

Part 3 · Grade by Grade

What matters at each grade.

3rd to 5th gradeYour player

Fun and many sports

No college scout is watching a 10 year old. Playing one sport too early leads to overuse injuries, and some kids quit by age 13. Spend about 80% of the time on other sports and being a good athlete.

6th to 8th gradeYour player

Master the basics

Do not pay for national exposure camps. Focus on shooting form, finishing with the weak hand and passing out of double teams.

9th and 10th gradeYour player

Earn your spot

Your high school team comes first. Varsity minutes come from defense, rebounding and taking care of the ball, not 30 foot step back shots.

11th and 12th gradeYour player

Real recruiting and a clear role

Recruiting happens through real game film and real relationships with coaches. Play where you get 15 or more real minutes, not 3 minutes on a big name team's bench.
