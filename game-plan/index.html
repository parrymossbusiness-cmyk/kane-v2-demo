PRIORITY 2 OF 3 · EXAMPLE

Finish through contact.

Why: You get to the rim and lose the ball. That isn’t a strength problem yet. It’s a decision problem: you decide to shoot before you decide to absorb.

This week: Fifty finishes a day off two feet, with a parent or partner bumping on every rep. Count makes, not attempts.

Checkpoints: Day 30, 35 of 50 with contact. Day 60, 40 of 50. Day 90, taking the contact in games without looking for the whistle.

Parent’s role: Hold the pad. Count out loud. Say nothing about the misses.

A FEW QUICK ANSWERS

Before you decide.

Do I need the Audit first?

No. The Game Plan starts with its own intake and film review. If you’ve already had a Future Success Audit, Coach already knows your player.

What ages and levels is it for?

Girls and boys in middle school through high school, playing school, club or AAU basketball. For a middle schooler the plan leans toward habits, coachability and love of the game. For a high schooler, toward role, minutes and what the next level actually requires.

What if we don’t have game film?

Phone video from the stands is enough: ten minutes of a game or a practice. If you have none, Coach writes from the intake and asks you for a short workout clip before the day-30 call.

Is my child on the calls?

The calls are built for the parent. If your player is 14 or older and wants to be on the day-90 call, Coach welcomes it.

How is this different from the Development System?

The Development System is three months of weekly calls: Coach is in the room every week. The Game Plan is the plan plus two checkpoints: you run it. If you want Coach every week, the Development System is the better fit.

How soon do we get the plan?

Within seven days of your intake coming back. Most families have their plan inside two weeks of enrolling.
