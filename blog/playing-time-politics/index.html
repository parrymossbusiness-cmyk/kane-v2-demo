Playing Time

Why Your Kid Isn't Getting Playing Time (And What to Actually Do About It)

The short answer: playing time comes down to three possible causes, team politics, a bad fit for the role or system, or a real, specific development gap, and each one requires a completely different response. Most parents guess which one it is and respond to the wrong problem.

This is the single most common reason parents reach out to Coach Dennis Kane, and after 440 wins and 50 years of coaching, he's seen every version of it. The frustrating part isn't the bench time itself. It's not knowing why it's happening, so parents either do nothing when they should act, or push hard on the wrong thing entirely.

Cause one: politics

Sometimes it really is politics: a coach favoring a player with family or program ties, a roster built around who's paying for what, or a personality conflict that has nothing to do with skill. This is real, but it's less common than parents assume, and it's the hardest one to fix directly. The right response is rarely confrontation. It's usually a calm conversation with the coach focused on your kid's development, not on minutes, plus a clear-eyed decision about whether the situation is worth staying in for another season.

Cause two: a bad fit

Sometimes a talented kid just doesn't fit what that specific team or coach needs right now, a shooting-heavy system with a kid who thrives driving to the rim, or a program that plays bigger lineups when your kid is undersized for their position. This isn't a knock on the player. It's a mismatch, and the fix is usually a team change, not a mindset change.

Cause three: a real development gap

And sometimes, the hardest one for parents to hear, the bench time is accurate. There's a specific, identifiable gap, conditioning, decision-making, a particular skill, that's genuinely holding the player back relative to the rest of the roster. This is actually the best-case scenario, because it's fixable with focused work, once someone tells you honestly that it's the real issue.

Why parents get this wrong

From the stands, all three causes look identical: a kid on the bench. Parents default to whichever explanation feels best, usually politics, because it doesn't require anything hard from the kid or the family. That's exactly backwards from how to actually solve it, and it's why an outside, honest read matters more than another conversation with other parents in the stands.

That read is what the AAU Situation Audit is built to deliver: a direct call with Coach Kane, a real diagnosis of which of the three causes is actually driving your kid's playing time, and three clear priorities for what to do next.
